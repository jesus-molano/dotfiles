# Writing for Agents

Write for execution, not persuasion. Lead with the objective, scope, constraints, and definition of done. State authority boundaries and forbidden actions explicitly. Give ordered steps with observable checkpoints, exact paths or commands when stable, and decision rules for ambiguity.

Separate facts, assumptions, and questions. Prefer concise imperative language, concrete examples, and links to canonical sources. Include validation and handoff expectations. Avoid vague verbs such as “handle” or “ensure” unless the measurable result follows. Never embed credentials, tokens, private keys, or environment-file contents; refer to approved secret-handling mechanisms instead.
